Amazon Beat Q1 Consensus by 70%. Strip Out the Mark-to-Market and It Was Roughly In Line.

Amazon's reported EPS has stopped describing Amazon. Q1 2026 printed $2.78 against a $1.63 estimate — and contained $16.8B of pre-tax gains on Anthropic securities booked in non-operating income. Q3 2025 carried $9.5B of the same alongside $4.3B of one-off charges running the other way. Thursday, the only clean read is operating income against the guided $20–24B band, and the only number that changes the thesis is capex — which now consumes 99% of operating cash flow.

Are we expecting a beat? Expect an operating beat — and ignore the EPS headline

Operating income has come in above the guided ceiling in four of the last six quarters, inside the band in the other two, and never below the floor. Revenue has exceeded the guided ceiling five quarters running. Against a $20.0–24.0B guide and $23.6B consensus, the pattern points to a beat.

The EPS line will not tell you. Q1 reported $2.78 against a $1.63 estimate, of which roughly $1.16 to $1.22 was an Anthropic mark. Whatever prints Thursday, subtract the non-operating line before calling it a beat, and anchor to operating income against the guided band.

The Setup

Four Things That Are True Going In

Figures drawn from Amazon's own earnings release and 10-Q are marked filed. Market data and consensus are from named third-party trackers. My arithmetic is marked computed.

01

The Operating Business Is Genuinely Strong

Q1 2026, from the filed release: net sales $181,519M, up 17%; operating income $23,852M, up 30%; operating margin 13.1%, the highest Amazon has ever reported.[1] North America margin went from 6.3% to 7.9%. Advertising grew 24% to $17,243M, now above $70B trailing. Unit growth was 15%, the fastest since the tail of the lockdown period. None of that is in dispute, and none of it depends on the accounting question below.

02

But Reported EPS Is Now an Investment Mark

That same quarter reported $2.78 of diluted EPS against a $1.63 consensus. Per the 10-Q, total other income was $15,647M against $2,749M a year earlier, comprising $4.5B of reclassification gains on converted Anthropic notes and $12.3B of upward fair-value adjustments on Anthropic preferred stock.[2] Applying a 21% to 25% tax rate, that mark contributed roughly $1.16 to $1.22 per share computed — meaning underlying EPS was near $1.56 to $1.62 against a $1.63 estimate. The 70% beat was, operationally, a roughly in-line quarter.

03

Free Cash Flow Has Effectively Gone to Zero

Trailing operating cash flow was $148,531M, up 30% — an excellent number. Trailing capex was $147,299M, up 67%. Free cash flow is therefore $1,232M, down from $25,925M.[1] Capex is consuming 99.2% of operating cash flow computed. The gap is being funded on the balance sheet: long-term debt went from $65,648M to $119,074M in a single quarter,[2] followed by C$14.0B of notes in June and a further multi-tranche dollar issue in July.[3]

04

The Contracted Demand Behind It Is Real and Large

This is the strongest counter to the capex worry. AWS backlog stood at $364B with a 5.5-year weighted average life, and management confirmed that figure excludes the more than $100B Anthropic commitment.[2] The OpenAI arrangement is a $38.0B original commitment expanded by $100.0B over eight years — note that the "$50B OpenAI-AWS deal" figure circulating in the press does not match the filing. Amazon's chips business is above a $20B run rate growing triple digits, with more than $225B of Trainium revenue commitments, Trainium2 "largely sold out" and Trainium3 "nearly fully subscribed."[1]

Amazon holds a large Anthropic position carried at fair value, with changes running through non-operating income. Every quarter the mark moves, reported EPS moves with it, and the move has nothing to do with retail, advertising or AWS.

The track record on this measure is remarkable and much more informative than the EPS column. Operating income has come in above the guided ceiling in four of the last six quarters, inside the band in the other two, and never below the floor. Revenue has exceeded the guided ceiling in five consecutive quarters. That is the pattern worth extrapolating, not the EPS surprises.

Two data traps worth naming. First, at least one widely syndicated tracker publishes a Q2 2026 revenue consensus of $188.7B — below Amazon's own guidance floor of $194.0B. That figure is unusable and is not used here; the usable cluster is $196.0B to $196.8B. Second, a "BofA raises Amazon $145 → $165" headline circulated in July; the article body describes retail sell-through data for an unrelated consumer product, and the price levels are incompatible with both AMZN at $231 and BofA's own $310 target. It is not an Amazon note and is not cited anywhere on this page.

Q1 2026 AWS: $37,587M of revenue, $14,161M of operating income, a 37.7% margin, roughly a $150B annualised run rate, with a $2B sequential increase — the largest Q4-to-Q1 increase in AWS history. AI services are above a $15B annualised run rate.[1]

The Competitive Frame — Read the Quarters Carefully

These are not the same period, and comparing them naively is the single most common error in cloud commentary. AWS +28% and Azure +40% (39% constant currency) are both the March 2026 quarter.[6] Google Cloud +81.8%, to $24,768M, is the June 2026 quarter, reported July 22.[7] AWS and Azure both report this week; Google's number is a quarter ahead.

The read-across that matters more than the growth ranking: Alphabet delivered 81.8% cloud growth with cloud operating income up 212%, and the stock fell roughly 15% — on its capex raise.[8] In this tape, cloud growth does not buy forgiveness for capital intensity. Amazon reports two days after Microsoft into exactly that setup, with the additional problem that its free cash flow is already near zero.

Where the sell side actually disagrees. UBS cut its target from $333 to $305 on July 28 while raising its growth estimates — it models AWS at +36% in H2 2026 against a street at +31%, and +48% in 2027 against a street at +30%, with 2027 operating income roughly 49% above consensus. The cut is entirely about higher 2027 capex from component prices, not about demand.[9] Bank of America separately expects Amazon to raise its 2026 capex guide and models AWS at +33%.

What Would Make This Wrong

Risks in Both Directions

A capex raise into a hostile tape

Med-High

Alphabet reported 81.8% cloud growth and fell about 15% on its capex raise. Amazon has the same setup with worse cash optics: free cash flow at $1.2B and long-term debt that nearly doubled in a quarter. Bank of America expects the 2026 capex guide to be raised.

Offset: a $364B backlog excluding a further $100B-plus Anthropic commitment is contracted demand, not a hope.

The mark reverses

Medium

Fair-value accounting cuts both ways. A downward Anthropic revaluation would push reported EPS below consensus while the business performs exactly as guided, and the first headline would read as a miss. Microsoft has already run this experiment with OpenAI: −$0.41 one quarter, +$1.02 the next.

Defence: anchor to operating income against the $20–24B band, which is unaffected.

The circularity

Medium

Amazon invests in Anthropic, books gains on that investment in earnings, and Anthropic commits to buy Amazon compute that shows up in backlog. Each leg is disclosed and legitimate. Taken together they make both the earnings line and the backlog line partially self-referential, and that deserves saying plainly.

Scale check: the $364B backlog explicitly excludes the Anthropic commitment, which limits the double-count on that specific measure.

AWS decelerates instead

Medium

Six quarters of acceleration is a strong trend and a high bar. UBS models +36% for the second half against a street at +31%. If AWS prints below 28%, the reacceleration narrative breaks at the same moment the capex bill arrives.

Support: Trainium2 largely sold out, Trainium3 nearly fully subscribed, and a $2B sequential increase that was the largest Q4-to-Q1 in AWS history.

Upside: operating income has never missed the floor

Low-Med

Cuts both ways. Operating income has landed above the guided ceiling in four of the last six quarters and inside the band in the other two. Revenue has beaten the guided ceiling five quarters running. The stock is roughly 17% below its 52-week high with a tight $313 consensus target.

Note: UBS cut its target while raising growth estimates — the bear case here is about capital intensity, not demand.
